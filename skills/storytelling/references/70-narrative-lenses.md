# Narrative lenses

Load this reference when the user requests a named lens or the shared narrative model
cannot localize a named structural problem. Use the requested lens or choose from the
symptom. Do not apply the catalog as a checklist.

## Lens map

### Broken causality: Aristotle

- Ask which consequences must change if an event changes
- Use for unity of action, reversal, recognition, and endings
- Do not treat every story as action-centered tragedy

### Anecdote without a point: Labov

- Ask where the telling reveals what the event means to the teller
- Use for oral stories, remembered events, and testimony
- Do not turn observed functions into a mandatory sequence

### Ineffective revelation: Genette

- Ask who tells, who perceives, and when information becomes available
- Use for order, duration, frequency, voice, focalization, and narrative levels
- Do not stop at technical analysis without making a narrative decision

### Recurrent conventional functions: Propp

- Ask what function an event performs regardless of its appearance
- Use for wonder tales and variants within one story family
- Do not present one morphology as a universal grammar

### Misplaced climax or uniform intensity: Freytag

- Ask where dramatic energy changes direction
- Use for work organized around a climax
- Do not force every story into a symmetrical pyramid

### Quest with empty thresholds: Hero's journey

- Ask which thresholds change a person's relation to the world
- Use for quests, departure, return, and transformation
- Do not universalize an individual heroic model

### Artificial conflict: Kishotenketsu

- Ask what new element changes the relation among what was already present
- Use for development through contrast, revelation, and integration
- Do not reduce the turn to a mechanical plot twist

### Flattened collective story: Carrier bag or dialogism

- Ask what the story gathers and whether each voice keeps its own logic
- Use for collective, accumulative, relational, or polyphonic work
- Do not confuse the absence of one hero with the absence of form

### Disoriented audience: Event indexing

- Track changes in time, space, attention carrier, causality, and intention
- Ask whether the audience can update its situation model
- Do not repair a deliberate rupture merely because it disrupts continuity

### Weak audience inhabitation: Narrative transportation

- Examine attention, imagery, emotion, and presence in the narrated world
- Ask what prevents the audience from inhabiting the offered experience
- Do not confuse immersion with quality, truth, or moral value

### Technical feature dump: Problem, behavior, value

- Trace before-state, friction, insight, artifact behavior, changed work, outcome, and
  value
- Ask whether the audience can explain why the subject exists and how it creates value
- For reconstruction, use [explain through story](45-explain-through-story.md) as the
  authoritative branch. For diagnosis, keep the current branch
- Do not turn a value hypothesis into a founder story or marketing claim
