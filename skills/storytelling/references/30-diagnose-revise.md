# Diagnose and revise

Use this branch when a narrative already exists.

## Principle

A strong revision does not merely decide whether the work is good.

It locates where attention, causality, credibility, voice, evidence, or promise breaks.

## 1. Read before correcting

Complete a first reading without rewriting.

Identify:

- apparent intention;
- audience promise;
- movement;
- living elements;
- non-negotiables;
- points where the experience changes;
- points where the experience dissipates.

Do not apply an external model yet.

**Completion criterion**

The work can be described according to its own logic before being evaluated.

## 2. Diagnose by layer

Examine in this order.

When the weakness concerns how a craft choice changes audience attention,
understanding, anticipation, emotion, or participation, read
[narrative mechanisms](05-narrative-mechanisms.md). Locate the earliest broken link
between craft choice, cue, audience operation, state shift, and story function.

### Contract

- Is the promised work the work being delivered?
- Is the truth contract clear?
- Does the intervention respect the author's voice and limits?

### Movement

- Does something matter?
- Does a force advance attention?
- Do the units create a shift?
- Is the change prepared?

### Coherence

- Are causes, motivations, conditions, and consequences legible?
- Are the associations in a fragmented work deliberate?
- Do coincidences replace decisions?

### Attention carrier and relationships

- Who or what carries attention?
- Does that entity have the agency required by the form?
- Do relationships reveal different facets?
- Does power move credibly?

### Information

- Is point of view coherent?
- Does the audience learn something too early, too late, or twice?
- Does a revelation genuinely reconfigure what came before?
- Does the work explain what the audience already understands?

### Scenes and sequences

- Does every unit have a function?
- Is the entry state different from the exit state?
- Do scenes begin too early or end too late?
- Is a quiet scene being mistaken for an inert scene?

### Emotion and theme

- Are emotions earned?
- Does the work demand a reaction the material does not support?
- Is theme dramatized or explained?
- Has productive ambiguity been closed by reflex?

### Voice, style, and medium

- Does the voice remain singular?
- Does the style serve the experience?
- Is the medium being used?
- Does the work sound generically competent?

### Evidence and value

For factual or explanatory work:

- Does each consequential claim have the correct basis and status in the claim ledger?
- Does the narrative overstate origin, impact, or business value?
- Does the explanation connect subject causality or artifact behavior to outcome?
- Has a feature list replaced an account of why the subject matters?

**Completion criterion**

Every important weakness is tied to an identifiable passage or decision.

## 3. Rank interventions by leverage

Address first the issue that makes several later corrections unnecessary:

- wrong promise;
- indeterminate core;
- broken causality;
- unsuitable point of view;
- ineffective revelation structure;
- missing or unnecessary scene;
- unsupported evidence or value claim.

Do not polish sentences if the scene should be removed.

**Completion criterion**

No more than three dominant problems explain most of the lost effect.

## 4. Propose the smallest correction

For each dominant problem, provide:

- diagnosis;
- exact location;
- current effect;
- intended effect;
- smallest plausible correction;
- a more ambitious option only when it opens a genuinely different direction;
- the tradeoff of each option.

If a mechanism is involved, repair its earliest broken link. Do not intensify later
devices to compensate for a missing cue, context, or audience reason.

**Completion criterion**

The author can choose an intervention while understanding its narrative cost.

## 5. Revise only when authorized

If the user requested diagnosis, explanation, or options without revision, deliver the
diagnosis after Step 4 and stop. Do not create a revised version.

When revision is authorized, respect the intervention contract.

In surgical revision:

- preserve living vocabulary;
- preserve irregularities that carry voice;
- avoid normalizing rhythm;
- do not make effective subtext explicit;
- do not repair what is merely unusual;
- preserve qualified uncertainty in factual work.

Load the target medium reference from `SKILL.md` only when the diagnosis reaches a
medium-specific problem. Load `references/50-voice-style.md` only when the
diagnosis reaches voice or style.

**Completion criterion**

For diagnosis-only work, the deliverable stops at the authorized diagnosis and options.
For revision work, the revised version resolves the diagnosis without becoming the
skill's text.
