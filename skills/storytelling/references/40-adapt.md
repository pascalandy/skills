# Adapt a story

Use this branch when a story changes medium, length, audience, or telling situation.

## 1. Separate the invariant from its realization

Identify what must survive:

- the core;
- the promise;
- the relationships;
- the choices;
- the change;
- essential images or motifs;
- the truth contract;
- the non-negotiables.

Separate these invariants from techniques specific to the source version.

**Completion criterion**

It is possible to state what would make the adaptation remain the same story.

## 2. Compare the affordances of both forms

For the source and target, identify:

- what each shows easily;
- what each hides easily;
- its relationship to time;
- its relationship to interiority;
- its handling of space;
- its relationship with the audience;
- its unit of rhythm;
- its capacity for repetition;
- its duration and format constraints.

Load the target medium reference selected in `SKILL.md`. Also load the source medium
family when its devices or affordances are not already clear. Load
[voice and style](50-voice-style.md) only when voice or style must survive the change.

**Completion criterion**

The gains and losses of the change in form are explicit.

## 3. Translate functions, not devices

Read [narrative mechanisms](05-narrative-mechanisms.md) when an important source
device carries audience movement. Identify its cue, audience operation, and function
before choosing a target realization.

For every important source device, ask:

- What narrative function does it perform?
- What target-medium device can perform that function?
- Should it be replaced, condensed, moved, or removed?

Examples:

- inner thought may become behavior, voice, montage, or silence;
- description may become composition, sound, setting, or framing;
- a page turn may become a cut, pause, return, or interaction;
- a refrain may become a visual motif or return structure.

**Completion criterion**

Every important transformation has an equivalent function or an acknowledged loss.

## 4. Recompose the trajectory

Do not mechanically transpose the same units.

Redefine:

- entry and exit points;
- sequences;
- revelations;
- omissions;
- repetitions;
- rhythm;
- ending.

**Completion criterion**

The target version works in its own medium without requiring the source to be
understood.

## 5. Test the right kind of fidelity

Fidelity does not mean preserving every event.

Check fidelity to:

- the core;
- the promise;
- the relationships;
- the truth contract;
- the intended experience.

**Completion criterion**

Departures are intentional, explainable, and productive.

## 6. Complete the requested adaptation level

If the user requested an adaptation plan, map, treatment, outline, or decision, produce
that deliverable and stop.

If the user requested a finished target telling, return to the branch sequence in
`SKILL.md` and enter **Write** only after the adaptation choices above are stable.

**Completion criterion**

The requested planning artifact is complete, or the target core, trajectory, medium
choices, gains, losses, and fidelity conditions are explicit enough for **Write** to
produce the finished target telling without reopening adaptation decisions.
