# Explain a concept or process: reconstruct causality

Use this reference for a natural phenomenon, concept, historical development, social
process, or other subject that is not a designed intervention.

## 1. Establish the evidence boundary

Build the claim ledger from sources that can establish the subject's causal behavior,
history, limits, and unresolved questions. Separate the history of a discovery from
the phenomenon or concept discovered.

**Completion criterion**

Every consequential claim has a basis, status, and source. Historical agency and the
subject's causal model remain distinct.

## 2. Define the audience's learning job

Identify what the audience already believes, what it needs to understand or decide,
which misconception blocks it, and what depth of causal explanation the request
requires.

**Completion criterion**

The intended audience shift determines the explanation's depth and exclusions.

## 3. Build the causal model

Identify only what applies:

- the starting condition or state
- the relevant entities, parts, or participants
- the forces, rules, incentives, or relations among them
- the sequence, cycle, feedback, or accumulation that carries change
- the resulting state or observable effect
- the limits, exceptions, competing explanations, and unknowns

Do not assign intention, emotion, or agency to a process merely to make it feel like a
story.

**Completion criterion**

The audience's required level of change can be traced through the subject's causal
model from starting condition to result without a missing link.

## 4. Choose a truthful carrier

Possible carriers include:

- one representative instance moving through the process
- a before-and-after mental model
- an inquiry moving from confusion to explanation
- a change of scale that reveals the same causal relation differently
- a discovery history when the sources support its chronology and agency
- several perspectives when the process is social or collective

The carrier organizes attention. It does not become a fictional protagonist unless the
truth contract permits invention.

**Completion criterion**

The carrier makes the causal model easier to follow without changing what the evidence
supports.

## 5. Bound analogies and demonstrations

Use an analogy only when it preserves the relation being explained. State where the
analogy stops matching. Use demonstrations as evidence or model checks, not as
decoration.

**Completion criterion**

Every analogy maps a named relation, every mismatch is disclosed, and each
demonstration supports a claim the audience needs.

## Completion gate

Reconstruction is sound when the causal model has no unsupported jump, the carrier
does not invent agency, and important limits remain visible. Then read
[narrative delivery](48-explain-deliver.md).
