# Lens 7 — Decision-Making & Cognitive Biases

## When to apply this lens

You are making a decision (or watching one being made) and the question
is whether the *thinking process itself* is sound. Symptoms:

- The decision involves uncertainty and your gut is loud.
- Someone is supremely confident in a domain they don't know well.
- You're defending a choice partly because you've already invested in it.
- Two people are interpreting the same data oppositely.
- Strong opinions about the next 1 year vs. weak opinions about the next 10.
- You're asking "should I keep going?" and feeling stuck.

**Posture:** these laws apply to *all* decisions — code, hiring, business,
relationships, career. They are the most domain-portable of the
collection. The job of this lens is to slow down a decision long enough
to see whether the obvious answer is the biased answer.

## How to use

1. **Inversion first.** When the decision feels stuck, ask "what would
   guarantee the worst outcome?" and remove those causes. This often
   resolves the question without needing the positive answer.
2. **Sunk cost check.** If you were starting today, knowing what you know
   now, would you choose this path? If no, the past investment is not
   a reason to continue.
3. **Pareto scan.** Which 20% of options / inputs / risks dominate? Spend
   reasoning effort proportional to that, not to noise.
4. **Confirmation-bias check.** What evidence would change your mind? If
   you can't name any, you're not deciding — you're rationalizing.
5. **Dunning-Kruger check.** How confident are you, and how deep is your
   actual experience here? Calibrate down when the gap is large.
6. **Lindy check** (for tools, ideas, institutions). Old things that have
   survived are likely to keep surviving; new things that have survived a
   short time are not yet evidence of much.

## Common compositions

- **Inversion + Sunk Cost** — the canonical "should I keep going?" pair.
- **First Principles + Occam** — strip a problem to its fundamentals, then prefer the simpler explanation.
- **Confirmation Bias + Goodhart (Lens 3)** — defending a metric you set yourself.
- **Hanlon + Dunning-Kruger** — most "bad behavior" is incompetence, often by people who don't know they're incompetent.
- **Hype Cycle + Lindy** — what's loud right now (hype) vs what's been quietly working for decades (Lindy).
- **Pareto + Amdahl (Lens 5)** — both say: most of the gain is in 20% of the work.
- **Cunningham's Law** — when you're stuck and need feedback, propose a confidently wrong answer.

This lens composes with **every other lens** in the collection — bias is
the meta-problem behind almost every misapplication of the technical laws.
