# Write narrative

Use this branch when the narrative core and trajectory are stable and a finished
narrative unit or complete telling must be produced in the target medium.

## 1. Set the scale and function

Load the target medium reference selected in `SKILL.md`, then choose units native to
that medium. Examples include:

- phrases, paragraphs, scenes, sequences, or chapters in prose
- moments, segments, verses, refrains, or returns in spoken and audio work
- beats, shots, scenes, panels, strips, pages, or stage actions in visual work
- player actions, encounters, states, loops, choices, or branches in interactive work
- sections, slides, examples, or demonstration steps in explanatory work

Use the smallest unit that carries a meaningful change. Do not force every medium into
beats, scenes, and sequences.

Establish:

- the audience state on entry
- the world or relationship state on entry
- the force carrying the unit
- the audience state on exit
- the world or relationship state on exit
- why this unit must exist

A unit may serve atmosphere, but that effect must be intentional.

**Completion criterion**

The scale matches the deliverable, and each planned unit has an entry state, exit
state, and necessary function.

## 2. Choose the local engine

Choose an engine for each unit. A longer telling may change engines when the transition
serves its movement. Engines include:

- an objective and resistance
- a question
- a discovery
- a negotiation
- a ritual
- an anticipation
- a seduction
- an observation
- an accumulation
- a contrast
- a presence or absence
- a recontextualization

Do not add an argument merely to manufacture energy.

Read [narrative mechanisms](05-narrative-mechanisms.md). For each major unit, connect
the engine to the audience operation that keeps attention moving, then choose the
smallest mechanism set that can produce it in the target medium.

**Completion criterion**

The engine explains why attention keeps moving, and each major mechanism has an
observable cue, intended audience operation, and failure condition.

## 3. Build the progression

Between units, use relations such as:

- action → reaction
- offer → response
- perception → interpretation
- tactic → result
- question → partial information
- gesture → power shift
- repetition → variation
- expectation → rupture
- revelation → rereading
- choice → consequence
- state → available action
- return → transformed meaning

At larger scales, apply the same logic between groups of units. Avoid units that change
no possibility, understanding, relationship, or felt experience.

**Completion criterion**

Every unit prepares, causes, or reconfigures the next.

## 4. Distribute information

Decide deliberately:

- what the audience knows
- what the attention carrier knows
- what every other relevant entity knows
- what is hidden
- what is misunderstood
- what changes meaning
- what should remain outside the frame

Distinguish suspense, mystery, surprise, and dramatic irony.

**Completion criterion**

Each piece of information arrives when it best serves the intended effect.

## 5. Realize the telling through the medium

Use the target medium reference already loaded. Load `references/50-voice-style.md`
only when voice or style requires separate handling.

Do not compensate for a medium's constraints with lazy explanation.

**Completion criterion**

The telling uses at least one capability specific to the medium.

## 6. Revise the telling immediately

Check:

- necessity
- movement
- point of view
- continuity
- subtext
- rhythm
- exposition
- emotion
- voice
- exit

Remove explanation that merely repeats what action, image, sound, or silence already
makes perceptible.

Keep direct narration when it is stronger than dramatization.

**Completion criterion**

The requested telling is complete at its chosen scale, produces its intended effect
without major redundancy, and does not betray the author's voice.
