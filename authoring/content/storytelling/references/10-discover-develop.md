# Discover and develop

Use this branch when material exists but the story does not yet have a stable form.

Use the [narrative brief](../assets/00-narrative-brief.md) when the work needs a durable
brief. Use the [story bible](../assets/10-story-bible.md) only when continuity must be
maintained across a long work.

## 1. Inventory the living material

Identify what is already present:

- images;
- moments;
- people;
- relationships;
- facts;
- phrases;
- voice;
- emotions;
- contradictions;
- motifs;
- a suspected beginning or ending;
- source artifacts;
- constraints;
- non-negotiables.

Do not introduce new material before reflecting what already has energy.

**Completion criterion**

Every clearly important element is named without being displaced by the skill's ideas.

## 2. Form a provisional core

Offer a provisional reading of:

- what carries attention;
- what matters;
- what applies pressure;
- what could change;
- the human question;
- the tellability;
- the audience promise.

Present this as an inference when the author has not confirmed it.

**Completion criterion**

The core fits in a short paragraph and respects every non-negotiable element.

## 3. Identify productive indeterminacy

Distinguish:

- what is genuinely missing;
- what may remain ambiguous;
- what is contradictory;
- what requires a decision;
- what can be discovered through writing.

Do not turn every uncertainty into a question for the author.

**Completion criterion**

Blocking decisions are separated from ambiguities that give the story life.

## 4. Explore genuinely different directions

When alternatives are useful, propose no more than three.

Vary structural dimensions such as:

- attention carrier;
- scale;
- engine of movement;
- starting point;
- order of information;
- kind of change;
- degree of resolution;
- relationship between personal and collective experience.

Do not offer three cosmetic versions of the same story.

When the alternatives depend on different audience operations rather than only
different subject matter, read
[narrative mechanisms](05-narrative-mechanisms.md). Use the smallest mechanism set
that makes each direction causally distinct.

For each direction, state:

- its promise;
- what it centers;
- what it sacrifices;
- its main risk;
- what it requires from the medium.

**Completion criterion**

Each direction creates a different audience experience, and its tradeoffs are visible.

## 5. Build the trajectory

Once a direction is chosen, organize units of movement.

If the causal link between a unit's craft choice and audience shift remains unclear,
read [narrative mechanisms](05-narrative-mechanisms.md) if it is not already loaded.
Do not add devices before locating the missing audience operation.

For each unit, define:

- entry state;
- active force;
- decision, perception, or event;
- consequence;
- exit state;
- question passed to the next unit.

Use a known structure only when it illuminates this particular story.

**Completion criterion**

Every unit shifts the situation, relationship, understanding, capability, or felt
experience.

## 6. Prepare the requested deliverable or transition

Depending on the assignment, prepare only what the current branch requires:

- premise;
- narrative brief;
- synopsis;
- sequence map;
- character or attention-carrier profile;
- chronology;
- story bible;
- ending options;
- discovery-writing plan.

If `SKILL.md` routes the work into **Write** or **Adapt**, prepare the smallest handoff
that satisfies the next branch's entry conditions. Do not present that handoff as the
final deliverable unless the user requested it.

**Completion criterion**

The result either satisfies the request or makes the permitted next branch executable
without closing useful possibilities.
