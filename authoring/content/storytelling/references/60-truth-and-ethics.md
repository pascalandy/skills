# Narrative truth and ethics

Load this reference for factual, autobiographical, documentary, source-based, hybrid,
or other people's lived experience.

Use the claim ledger from `references/00-narrative-model.md` for every consequential
claim. The rules below refine what counts as a truthful basis and a proportionate
claim.

## Truth contract

Establish which assertions and transformations are permitted:

- presentation as established fact
- quotation
- paraphrase
- memory
- observation
- inference
- reconstruction
- condensation
- anonymization
- combination
- invention

In factual work, invention is never permitted by default.

## Source basis

Record the specific source behind a **Stated**, **Observed**, or **Reconstructed**
basis:

- direct observation
- personal memory
- another person's testimony
- creator statement
- current documentation
- code or demonstrated behavior
- issue or discussion
- historical record
- recording
- data
- reconstruction
- interpretation
- rumor

Treat interpretation and rumor as such. Use a **None** basis and **Unknown** status when
the available material does not answer a question.

## Source time and version

For evolving artifacts, separate:

- what was true at creation;
- what a creator intended at a particular time;
- what the current implementation does;
- what users report in a particular version;
- what remains planned or aspirational.

An old talk may be excellent evidence of origin and poor evidence of current behavior.
A current README may describe behavior and omit the original motivation.

Do not silently reconcile temporal differences.

## Memory

A sincere memory may remain uncertain.

Preserve formulations such as:

- "as I remember it";
- "I believe";
- "as I understood it at the time";
- "accounts differ";
- "this detail could not be confirmed."

Do not fill gaps merely to improve flow.

## Dialogue and interiority

Do not invent:

- an exact quotation;
- a thought;
- a motive;
- an emotion;
- an intention;
- a causal sequence not established by the material.

Identify any reconstruction as reconstruction.

## Inference and business value

A technically plausible value chain is still an inference until the relevant context
and evidence support it.

Distinguish:

- a capability the artifact demonstrably enables;
- a workflow change that follows under stated conditions;
- an outcome observed by users;
- an organizational consequence inferred from that outcome;
- a quantified impact supported by measurement.

Do not convert potential value into realized value.

Do not invent:

- adoption;
- cost savings;
- revenue impact;
- risk reduction;
- productivity gains;
- user satisfaction;
- strategic importance.

Use conditional language or an explicit value hypothesis when evidence is incomplete.

## Condensation and composite subjects

Do not use composite people, teams, incidents, or chronologies without explicit
authorization.

When allowed, make the device transparent according to the medium's conventions.

## Real people

Consider:

- consent;
- privacy;
- dignity;
- safety;
- power relationships;
- foreseeable harm;
- right of response;
- ownership of the story;
- context removed;
- consequences after publication.

Legal permission alone does not establish narrative fairness.

## Trauma and vulnerability

Do not use suffering as a shortcut to importance.

Avoid:

- voyeurism;
- simplification;
- forced redemption;
- aestheticization of harm;
- reducing a person to their worst event;
- emotion the facts do not support.

## Collective and cultural stories

Do not assume every story belongs freely to the person who heard it.

Consider:

- authority to tell;
- responsibility to the community;
- cultural protocols;
- attribution;
- reciprocity;
- relational context;
- limits on distribution.

Do not automatically transpose a collective story into an individual hero model.

## Narrative truth

Making a story clearer does not grant permission to make it cleaner than it was.

Preserve when relevant:

- contradictions;
- uncertainty;
- asymmetry;
- silence;
- lack of resolution;
- multiple accounts;
- limits of memory;
- tradeoffs;
- failure;
- unknown impact.

## Final control

Before delivery, verify:

- every consequential claim has a basis and status;
- every inferred, unknown, or contradicted claim remains qualified;
- every reconstruction is marked;
- no real person receives an invented inner state;
- condensation does not change meaning;
- dates and versions are not silently collapsed;
- potential value is not presented as realized impact;
- risks and permissions were considered;
- the story was not removed from context merely to increase impact;
- emotion remains proportional to the material.
