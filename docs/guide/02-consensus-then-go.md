# Aim for consensus, then say go

My way of working comes down to two words. `feel-me` aligns the agent with what I want. `go` starts the work.

## End each request with `feel-me`

```text
I want to send a monthly newsletter to my clients.

feel-me
```

I do this for every request, even one that seems easy. A request always leaves gaps, and the agent fills them by guessing. `feel-me` makes it align with you before acting.

The agent changes nothing. It restates your goal and the problem to solve, then asks questions that only you can settle. Here, those concern the email tool, the list size, and who writes the content. A question about a decision includes:

- **FMO**: the change it suggests, concrete enough for you to act on, followed by the choices
- **CMO**: how things work today, after the choices, for reference

For a fact only you know, such as your list size, the agent asks a direct question with no FMO, choices, or recommendation. When the fact is a plain yes or no, so is the question.

## Answer until no questions remain

Each question has a number. The agent asks at most four per round, with the most important first. A decision has lettered choices and a recommendation marked 🟢. Reply in one line:

```text
1a, 2: 400 addresses, 3a
```

The agent applies your answers, rethinks its proposals, and asks again while a decision remains open. When none remain, it concludes:

```text
👍 Je n'ai plus de question.
```

That means "I have no questions left".

## Type `go`

```text
go
```

The agent carries out what you agreed on, without guessing. If something is wrong, say so instead of typing `go`, and it revises its proposals.

You can combine `feel-me` with a mode. Alignment comes first. The playbook follows when you say `go`:

```text
marketing ; launch. I'm launching a bookkeeping service by text message.

feel-me
```

**Pitfall:** typing `go` without reading the proposals. Agreement helps you catch a wrong idea while it still costs little to change.
